# Control Large Fields and Topology

## Objective

Turn a local perturbative or small-field RG analysis into a statement about the complete gauge-invariant measure. Treat exceptional configurations and topological sectors as proof obligations, not negligible prose.

## Workflow

1. Define small and large fields in gauge-invariant terms, such as plaquette curvature or orbit-distance conditions.
2. Decompose space into regular and exceptional blocks without selecting an unjustified global gauge slice.
3. Prove probability or activity bounds for exceptional blocks that are summable over locations, scales, and polymers.
4. Derive polymer or cluster estimates that retain explicit volume-uniform constants.
5. Classify boundary conditions and topological sectors relevant to the regulator.
6. Control tunneling, instanton-like sectors, torons, center flux, and global holonomy to the degree required by the target theorem.
7. Show that local coordinate charts cover all retained configurations or bound the complement quantitatively.
8. Propagate the exceptional-sector estimates through every RG step and verify total error summability.

## Required Evidence

- Gauge-invariant definition of exceptional events.
- A summable tail or activity estimate.
- Orbit-space coverage or an explicit atlas with overlap control.
- Treatment of boundary and topological sectors.
- Stability under blocking and reconstruction.
- Uniformity in volume and cutoff.

## Stop Conditions

Stop and label the result local, perturbative, or conditional if it holds only near the trivial connection, assumes a global gauge fixing, drops topological sectors, or uses a volume-dependent exceptional probability whose thermodynamic limit is uncontrolled.

## Output Contract

Return a sector decomposition, quantitative exceptional-set bounds, polymer summability conditions, topology ledger, and a list of assumptions still needed for full-measure control. Never promote small-field asymptotics alone to nonperturbative basin entry.
