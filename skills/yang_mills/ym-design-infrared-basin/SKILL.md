# Design Infrared Basin

## Aim

Specify a subset of effective theories whose membership can be checked at one scale, is stable under perturbations and RG iteration, and yields quantitative long-distance rigidity by an independent theorem.

## Candidate Mechanisms

Compare conditional Poincare or log-Sobolev inequalities, Dobrushin-type influence contraction, transfer-operator contraction, gauge-orbit coercivity, convergent polymer activities, and multiscale conditional mixing. State whether each mechanism concerns the physical Euclidean measure or only an auxiliary dynamics.

## Design Workflow

1. Define the effective-theory space and a metric or norm on interactions and observable transports.
2. Give a finite-scale membership predicate using local conditional distributions, polymer norms, or operator bounds.
3. Prove that membership is gauge invariant and uniform in volume.
4. Prove openness or quantitative perturbative stability.
5. Prove stability under one RG step, including reconstruction and error terms.
6. Derive an independent basin-to-rigidity theorem with an explicit physical decay rate.
7. Test massive, gapless, topological, finite-volume, and hidden-sector countermodels.

## Noncircularity Rules

Reject a basin defined by the desired continuum mass gap, by all-distance exponential clustering, or by an unknown infinite-volume spectral property merely renamed. Local conditional inequalities may be used only if their constants are independently verifiable and their iteration to physical observables is proved.

## Output Contract

Return the basin predicate, local verification protocol, stability radius, RG invariance statement, basin-to-rigidity theorem, physical rate formula, and falsification results. If any component is missing, label the basin a candidate rather than an established proof mechanism.
