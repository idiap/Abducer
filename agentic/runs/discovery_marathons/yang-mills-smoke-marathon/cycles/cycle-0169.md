# Reflection-Positivity Closed-Cone Topology

## Candidate Lemma

Let \(S_{a,L}\) be finite-volume/cutoff Euclidean Yang-Mills Schwinger functionals on a locked gauge-invariant positive-time observable algebra \(\mathcal A^{\mathrm{lock}}_+\). Suppose \(S_{a_n,L_n}\to S\) in the RG cutoff-removal topology \(\tau_{RG}\), every \(S_{a_n,L_n}\) is reflection-positive on the corresponding cutoff realization of \(\mathcal A^{\mathrm{lock}}_+\), and every finite OS quadratic probe is continuous for \(\tau_{RG}\) after the cutoff-to-continuum identification. Then \(S\) is OS-positive on \(\mathcal A^{\mathrm{lock}}_+\). Equivalently, the RG passage is valid for reflection positivity if \(\tau_{RG}\) dominates the initial topology generated by finite reflected quadratic forms.

## Definitions

- source object: a net \((S_{a,L})\) of regulated reflection-positive Schwinger functionals for gauge-invariant lattice/block observables, with cutoff realization maps \(I_{a,L}:\mathcal A^{\mathrm{lock}}_+\to\mathcal A_{a,L,+}\).
- target object: a continuum Schwinger functional \(S\) satisfying OS positivity on the same gauge-invariant positive-time algebra, hence eligible for the OS Hilbert-space reconstruction with positive Hamiltonian once the remaining OS axioms are verified.
- controlled interface: regulated reflection-positive Schwinger forms -> OS-positive continuum Schwinger functions, mediated by convergence of each finite quadratic form \(Q_{S_{a,L}}(I_{a,L}F,c)\to Q_S(F,c)\).
- same-theory condition: \(\mathcal A^{\mathrm{lock}}_+\) is fixed before taking limits; \(I_{a,L}\) approximates the identity on continuum gauge-invariant observables and no gauge fixing, quotient completion, or replacement by a smaller positive cone changes the observable theory.

## Assumptions

- For each finite list \(F_1,\dots,F_m\in\mathcal A^{\mathrm{lock}}_+\) and coefficients \(c\in\mathbb C^m\), the regulated quadratic form \(Q_{a,L}(F,c)\) is nonnegative.
- There is a cofinal cutoff sequence \((a_n,L_n)\) along which \(S_{a_n,L_n}\to S\) in \(\tau_{RG}\).
- OS-probe domination: for every finite probe \(P=(F_1,dots,F_m,c)\) there are \(\tau_{RG}\)-seminorms \(p_1,dots,p_N\) and a constant \(C_P\) such that \(|Q_T(P)|\le C_P\max_r p_r(T)\) for all differences of admissible Schwinger functionals \(T\).
- The cutoff realization maps commute with reflection in the limit: \(\Theta_{a,L}I_{a,L}F-I_{a,L}\Theta F\to0\) in the probe seminorms relevant to \(P\).
- The observable supports are initially taken a positive distance from the reflection plane; extension to boundary-touching probes is deferred to a separate trace/contact-term lemma.

## Equations

- finite OS probe: `Q_S(F,c)=\sum_{i,j=1}^m \overline{c_i}c_j\,S\big((\Theta F_i)F_j\big)`. Valid when: The products \((\Theta F_i)F_j\) are in the domain of the continuum Schwinger functional and the reflection map preserves the locked gauge-invariant algebra.
- RG domination of OS probe: `|Q_{S-S'}(F,c)|\le C_{F,c}\max_{1\le r\le N}p_r(S-S')`. Valid when: The cutoff-removal topology controls the reflected product observables generated by the chosen finite probe.
- closed-cone consequence: `Q_{S_{a_n,L_n}}(I_{a_n,L_n}F,c)\ge0,\quad Q_{S_{a_n,L_n}}(I_{a_n,L_n}F,c)\to Q_S(F,c)\;\Longrightarrow\;Q_S(F,c)\ge0`. Valid when: The same-theory identification and OS-probe domination both hold for the probe \((F,c)\).

## Attempted Derivation

- Fix a finite OS probe \(P=(F_1,\dots,F_m,c)\) in the locked positive-time gauge-invariant algebra, first with supports at distance \(\delta>0\) from the reflection plane.
- Use regulated reflection positivity to get \(Q_{a_n,L_n}(I_{a_n,L_n}F,c)\ge0\) for every cutoff in the cofinal sequence.
- Decompose the desired convergence as \(|Q_{a_n,L_n}(I_{a_n,L_n}F,c)-Q_S(F,c)|\le A_n(P)+B_n(P)\), where \(A_n\) measures Schwinger functional convergence on the reflected product observable and \(B_n\) measures the error of replacing the cutoff realization/reflection by the locked continuum observable.
- Apply OS-probe domination to force \(A_n(P)\to0\) from \(S_{a_n,L_n}\to S\) in \(\tau_{RG}\).
- Apply the same-theory condition and asymptotic commutation \(\Theta_{a,L}I_{a,L}\approx I_{a,L}\Theta\) to force \(B_n(P)\to0\).
- Conclude \(Q_S(F,c)=\lim_n Q_{a_n,L_n}(I_{a_n,L_n}F,c)\ge0\) for separated-support probes.
- Attempt to remove the separation restriction by choosing time-mollified approximants \(F_i^{(\delta)}\to F_i\) and passing \(\delta\downarrow0\).

Blocking gap: The proof still lacks the analytic domination estimate for all reflected gauge-invariant probes actually generated by the Yang-Mills RG construction. In particular, it is not known here that Balaban-style compactness controls nonlocal Wilson-loop products and reflection-plane trace limits with constants uniform enough to make \(\tau_{RG}\) dominate \(\tau_{OS}\).

## Falsifiers

- falsifier: Find a sequence of reflection-positive regulated Schwinger functionals converging in the advertised RG topology while a finite reflected Wilson-loop quadratic form converges to a negative value; this would show \(\tau_{RG}\) is too weak.
- falsifier: Show that the cutoff realization maps \(I_{a,L}\) do not asymptotically commute with time reflection for blocked gauge-invariant observables near \(t=0\), so that positivity is transferred to a different algebra rather than the intended theory.
- falsifier: Exhibit a reflection-plane contact term invisible for probes with support distance \(\delta>0\) but negative for a limiting positive-time observable; then away-from-plane positivity is not enough.
- falsifier: Prove that RG bounds control only local curvature insertions while the physical gauge-invariant algebra requires nonlocal loop observables whose reflected products are outside the controlled distribution space.

## Learning

Cycle 169 converts the branch's slogan into a precise domination theorem: OS positivity survives cutoff removal exactly when every finite reflected quadratic form is continuous for the actual RG topology after the same-theory observable identification. The formal closed-cone lemma is valid but no longer the bottleneck. The genuine Yang-Mills work is to prove reflected-probe domination for the locked gauge-invariant algebra, first away from the reflection plane and then through a trace/contact-term extension. This gives concrete falsifier tests rather than merely asking whether reflection positivity is preserved.
