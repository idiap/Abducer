# Reflection-Positivity Closed-Cone Topology

## Candidate Lemma

Mixed Wilson-collar trace bridge: assume regulated gauge-invariant Schwinger functionals are reflection-positive, converge on all fixed positive-time collars, and satisfy a no-new-boundary-renormalization trace modulus for every finite reflected Wilson pairing. Then the continuum Schwinger functional is OS-positive on the OS-closure of the same gauge-invariant algebra. The essential sharpening is that the trace modulus must be mixed in the OS quadratic form, not merely pointwise for single Wilson loops.

## Definitions

- source object: a cutoff net \(S_{a,L}\) of reflection-positive Euclidean Yang-Mills Schwinger functionals on a fixed gauge-invariant positive-time observable algebra \(\mathcal A_{a,+}\), including local mirror-flat composites and renormalized Wilson observables \(W_{R,a}(C)\).
- target object: a continuum Schwinger functional \(S\) on a gauge-invariant positive-time algebra \(\mathcal A_+\) whose finite reflected quadratic forms define an OS-positive pre-Hilbert space after quotienting null vectors.
- controlled interface: regulated reflection-positive Schwinger forms -> OS-positive continuum Schwinger functions, controlled by convergence of finite collar-separated reflected quadratic forms plus a boundary trace modulus as the collar width tends to zero.
- same-theory condition: the embeddings \(I_a:\mathcal A_+\to\mathcal A_{a,+}\) and collar maps \(T_\delta:\mathcal A_+\to\mathcal A_+\) use the same renormalized Wilson/local observables before and after the limit; no extra reflection-plane counterterm, cusp factor, boundary field, or algebra enlargement is allowed.
- \(T_\delta\) denotes a positive-time collaring/smoothing operation moving all supports or contours into \(t\ge \delta\), preserving gauge invariance and converging to the identity in the intended continuum observable topology.
- A mixed reflected Wilson pairing means an insertion of the form \(S(\Theta W_R(C_i)\,W_R(C_j))\), or a finite linear combination of such insertions, because OS positivity tests the product of a reflected observable with an unreflected one.

## Assumptions

- For every cutoff \((a,L)\), \(S_{a,L}\) is reflection-positive on \(\mathcal A_{a,+}\): all finite OS matrices \(S_{a,L}(\Theta I_aF_i,I_aF_j)\) are positive semidefinite.
- For each fixed \(\delta>0\), the RG/cutoff-removal topology is strong enough to give convergence of every finite collar-separated reflected quadratic form \(Q_{a,L}(T_\delta F,c)\to Q_S(T_\delta F,c)\).
- The collar maps are internal to the locked same-theory algebra: \(T_\delta F\in\mathcal A_+\), \(T_\delta F\to F\), and \(I_aT_\delta F\) is the regulated representative of the same observable, not a separately renormalized boundary observable.
- Mixed Wilson trace modulus: for each finite list \(F_i\) in a dense core generated by local mirror-flat composites and mirror-smooth Wilson loops, \(Q_S(T_\delta F,c)\to Q_S(F,c)\) as \(\delta\downarrow0\), uniformly enough to interchange the cutoff limit and the collar limit.
- No-new-boundary-renormalization: in every reflected pair \(\Theta C_i^\delta\cup C_j^\delta\), the renormalization needed as \(\delta\downarrow0\) is exactly the same multiplicative/local renormalization already assigned to the individual continuum observables; any residual boundary contact factor must tend to one.
- The dense core is OS-dense: every intended positive-time gauge-invariant observable is approximable in the seminorm \(\|F\|_{OS}^2=Q_S(F,1)\) by finite combinations from the core.

## Equations

- regulated positivity: `Q_{a,L}(F,c)=\sum_{i,j}\overline c_i c_j\,S_{a,L}(\Theta I_aF_i,I_aF_j)\ge0`. Valid when: Valid for finite lists \(F_i\in\mathcal A_+\) whose regulated representatives lie in the positive-time lattice/gauge-invariant algebra on which reflection positivity is established.
- fixed-collar closed-cone transfer: `Q_S(T_\delta F,c)=\lim_{a\downarrow0,L\uparrow\infty}Q_{a,L}(T_\delta F,c)\ge0\qquad(\delta>0)`. Valid when: Valid when the cutoff-removal topology controls each finite reflected quadratic form with supports separated from the reflection plane by the collar \(\delta\).
- mixed trace modulus: `\lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\left|Q_S(T_\delta F,c)-Q_S(F,c)\right|=0`. Valid when: Valid only if all mixed reflected insertions \(S(\Theta T_\delta F_i,T_\delta F_j)\) have boundary values at \(t=0\) in the same observable algebra.
- no boundary anomaly diagnostic: `\lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\limsup_{a\downarrow0}\left|B_a^{ij}(\delta)-1\right|=0,\quad B_a^{ij}(\delta):=\frac{\text{renormalization of }\Theta C_i^\delta\cup C_j^\delta}{\text{product of the prescribed renormalizations of }C_i^\delta\text{ and }C_j^\delta}`. Valid when: Valid as a same-theory test for Wilson-loop sectors; a nontrivial limit of \(B_a^{ij}\) is a falsifier of the bridge even if positivity could be restored in a modified boundary algebra.

## Attempted Derivation

- Fix a finite list \(F_1,\ldots,F_N\) in the gauge-invariant positive-time core and coefficients \(c_i\). For each \(\delta>0\), replace \(F_i\) by \(T_\delta F_i\), so every reflected product is separated from the reflection plane.
- By regulated reflection positivity, \(Q_{a,L}(T_\delta F,c)\ge0\) for every cutoff. By the RG-to-OS convergence assumption for fixed collars, pass to the cutoff limit to obtain \(Q_S(T_\delta F,c)\ge0\). This part is a valid closed-cone argument and contains no new Yang-Mills singular analysis.
- To remove the collar, try to write \(Q_S(F,c)=\lim_{\delta\downarrow0}Q_S(T_\delta F,c)\). For local mirror-flat composites, expand into finitely many Schwinger distributions and use \(T_\delta f\to f\); this gives the trace limit.
- For Wilson-loop generators, the same expansion is unavailable because \(\Theta W(C_i^\delta)W(C_j^\delta)\) can create a boundary contact in the reflected pair. The trace limit therefore requires a mixed, pairwise no-boundary-renormalization estimate, not just continuity of individual loop expectations.
- If the mixed trace modulus holds, then \(Q_S(F,c)\) is a limit of nonnegative numbers and hence nonnegative. OS-density of the core then extends positivity to the intended \(\mathcal A_+\).
- If a residual boundary factor \(B_a^{ij}\ne1\) appears, the derivation proves at most positivity for a different algebra with an inserted boundary renormalization; that violates the same-theory condition.

Blocking gap: The missing estimate is the mixed reflected Wilson collar trace: uniformly control \(S_{a,L}(\Theta W_{R,a}(C_i^\delta)W_{R,a}(C_j^\delta))\) as \(a\downarrow0\) and \(\delta\downarrow0\), proving that no cusp, intersection, hairpin, or reflection-plane counterterm appears in any OS matrix entry.

## Falsifiers

- A concrete hairpin Wilson-loop family whose reflected product has \(B_a^{ij}(\delta)\to B\ne1\) or a cusp anomalous divergence is a decisive falsifier of the same-theory bridge.
- The cutoff-removal topology controls individual Wilson-loop expectations but not mixed reflected expectations \(S(\Theta W(C_i)W(C_j))\); then closed-cone transfer is too weak to be valid for OS positivity.
- The collar map \(T_\delta\) changes the Wilson-loop renormalization scheme, for example by replacing a boundary-touching contour with a separately normalized smooth contour; positivity would then be proved for a different test algebra.
- Reflection positivity holds only for bare lattice Wilson observables, while the renormalized Wilson observables needed in the continuum require subtractions or counterterms that are not reflection-positive.
- OS-density fails: local mirror-flat composites and mirror-smooth Wilson collars do not approximate the intended gauge-invariant positive-time algebra in the OS seminorm.

## Learning

Cycle 178 identifies a sharper obstruction than previous cycles: Wilson-loop trace continuity must be checked on mixed reflected OS pairings, because \(Q(F,c)\) contains \(\Theta F_i\cdot F_j\). Even if each individual Wilson loop has a valid same-theory collar limit, the reflected product can develop a boundary cusp, hairpin, or contact renormalization at \(t=0\). Thus the real bridge lemma is not simply no-new-boundary-renormalization for single loops; it is no-new-boundary-renormalization for every finite OS matrix entry. The local mirror-flat sector remains plausibly solved by distributional continuity, so the next decisive falsifier or proof must come from mixed Wilson pairings.
