# Timeline Problem Literature LaTeX Translator

## Workflow

1. State the target problem in theorem/body form before giving history.
2. Organize literature evolution by conceptual pressure, not by decorative chronology.
3. Separate established literature, internal artifact evidence, and conjectural positioning.
4. Use citations for every external comparison and artifact references for internal timeline evidence.
5. End with the precise gap the current paper subgoal addresses and what it does not address.

## Epistemic Discipline

Preserve epistemic status: established literature, cited background, artifact-backed internal result, conjectural novelty, proof-obligation, validation-needed, promoted, demoted, or refuted. Cite source artifacts, literature references, KB IDs when available, and explicit unknowns.

Do not claim novelty until close comparators are named. Do not let broad target-problem prestige inflate a finite local contribution.

## Output Contract

Return: LaTeX introduction/background/related-work section, theorem-shaped target statement, literature stages, comparator families, citation keys or source paths, novelty boundary, non-claims, epistemic status, and validation handoff.

## Failure/Demotion

Demote the section if it lacks citations, confuses internal artifacts with literature, overclaims novelty, omits closest comparator families, or fails to connect the literature to the current finite paper subgoal.

## Validation Handoff

The LaTeX must compile, include citation commands or artifact references, and explicitly distinguish the main problem from the scoped contribution.
