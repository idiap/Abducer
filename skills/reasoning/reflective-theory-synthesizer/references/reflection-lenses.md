# Reflection lenses and transformation operators

Select lenses for their ability to change the live theory. For each lens, record
the exposed assumption, protected content, proposed mutation, and new risk.

## Ontological lens

Ask:

- What objects, relations, processes, structures, levels, or absences does the
  theory treat as fundamental?
- Which object may really be a relation, process, constraint, equivalence class,
  or emergent pattern?
- What entity or mediator is missing because the ontology cannot currently
  carry the required role?
- Which identity or difference is assumed rather than earned?

Possible mutations: introduce a latent carrier, replace object-first with
relation- or process-first structure, stratify levels, split an overloaded
entity, or revise identity conditions.

## Dialectical lens

Ask:

- What are the strongest opposed commitments, and what success is each
  protecting?
- Is the tension a contradiction, missing mediation, regime difference,
  underdetermination, or conflict of explanatory ideals?
- What would preserve the truth or success of both sides while transforming
  their relation?
- Should the opposition be resolved, relocated, stratified, or retained as a
  productive duality?

Possible mutations: mediation, determinate negation, regime decomposition,
level distinction, synthesis with preserved difference, or negative organizing
principle.

## Epistemological lens

Ask:

- What counts as knowing or understanding here: derivation, construction,
  mechanism, invariance, compression, prediction, control, or intelligibility?
- Which idealizations, access conditions, observables, witnesses, or proof
  standards determine what can be known?
- Does the framework confuse what exists with what its representation or method
  can detect?
- Which unknown is structural rather than merely missing data?

Possible mutations: new observable/interface, distinction between access and
existence, explicit idealization layer, revised evidence relation, or plural
standards of support.

## Explanatory lens

Ask:

- Does the theory explain by mechanism, law, constraint, symmetry, structure,
  emergence, unification, or derivation?
- Does it tell why the phenomenon occurs, or only redescribe regularities?
- Which explanatory dependency is missing or circular?
- What makes the explanation necessary rather than ad hoc?

Possible mutations: explicit mechanism, constraint-first explanation,
symmetry/invariance principle, multilevel explanation, or causal/structural
dependency reorganization.

## Representational and semantic lens

Ask:

- What does the current language make easy to state, and what does it render
  invisible or incoherent?
- Which distinctions are artifacts of coordinates, notation, encoding, gauge,
  quotient, or equivalence policy?
- Is a representation being mistaken for the represented object?
- What encoding/decoding or translation law would preserve meaning across
  frameworks?

Possible mutations: invariantization, quotient, coordinate-free reformulation,
new vocabulary, altered equivalence policy, functorial interface, or explicit
translation layer.

## Modal and counterfactual lens

Ask:

- Which features could vary while the phenomenon or theorem pressure remains?
- Which invariants are necessary, contingent, emergent, or regime-dependent?
- In which nearest alternative world does the proposed mechanism disappear or
  reverse?
- Does the theory explain robustness or only the actual case?

Possible mutations: distinguish necessary from enabling conditions, isolate an
invariant, introduce a regime parameter, or build a counterfactual family.

## Historical and genealogical lens

Ask:

- Which earlier problem, instrument, proof technique, or controversy caused the
  current concept to take this form?
- Which alternatives were excluded, forgotten, or made unintelligible?
- Is an inherited distinction still load-bearing, or only historically sticky?
- What earlier success must a successor concept preserve?

Possible mutations: recover an abandoned distinction, reinterpret a concept's
role, separate historical contingency from necessity, or reconstruct the
problem from a different lineage.

## Unificatory lens

Ask:

- Which separate cases, routes, scales, or constraints appear as instances of
  one principle?
- What common mechanism or structure does the theory reveal?
- Which differences must remain visible for the unification to be explanatory?
- Is the claimed compression genuine, or achieved by weakening the target or
  erasing boundary conditions?

Possible mutations: abstraction/interface discovery, common invariant,
categorification, shared mechanism, multi-regime theory, or dual description.

## Reflexive and methodological lens

Ask:

- Is the research workflow, representation, benchmark, proof style, or
  disciplinary norm manufacturing the apparent bottleneck?
- Which questions are never asked because the current framework cannot encode
  them?
- Is the search optimizing what is easy to verify rather than what matters?
- What change in method would expose a different conceptual landscape?

Possible mutations: change unit of analysis, invert the search order, preserve
plural theories longer, introduce a new reflection stage, or revise the
criterion of progress.

## Transformation tests

A mutation is substantive only if it changes at least one primitive,
identity/difference, relation, operation, mechanism, explanatory dependency,
equivalence policy, regime organization, or generative consequence. For every
mutation state:

- predecessor and trigger;
- structure preserved;
- structure changed or lost;
- new constructive resource or affordance;
- unification or understanding gain;
- residual contradiction and boundary case;
- next concept seed.

If none changes, record `no_change`; do not manufacture philosophical novelty.
