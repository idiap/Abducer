# Reflection-Positivity Closed-Cone Topology: boundary Gram triviality for Wilson collar collapse

## Candidate Lemma

Boundary Gram Triviality Criterion. Fix the locked gauge-invariant Wilson core and let \(F_i^\delta\) be reflected collar representatives of continuum observables \(F_i\) approaching the reflection plane. Suppose the fixed-collar OS matrices have a continuum limit and the remaining collar-collapse anomaly is multiplicative, normalized, and represented by a positive boundary Gram kernel \(B_{ij}=\langle b_i,b_j\rangle\) with \(B_{ii}=1\). Then the passage from the positive collar-limit matrices \(N=B\circ M\) to the intended same-theory OS matrices \(M\) is valid for every finite family only if \(B\) has rank one on every finite family. Equivalently, the logarithmic cross-ratio \(K_{ik;jl}\) vanishes and the boundary Hilbert sector generated by reflected Wilson hairpins is one-dimensional. Any non-rank-one \(B\), for example \(|B_{12}|<1\), is a same-theory falsifier, not merely a removable normalization.

## Definitions

- source object: regulated reflection-positive Schwinger forms \(S_{a,L}\) on a fixed gauge-invariant test algebra, with Wilson collar representatives \(F_i^\delta\subset\{t>\delta\}\) and finite OS matrices obtained after RG continuum passage at fixed \(\delta>0\).
- target object: OS-positive continuum Schwinger functions \(S\) on the full locked gauge-invariant algebra, including Wilson observables whose supports have valid boundary traces at the reflection plane.
- controlled interface: regulated reflection-positive Schwinger forms -> OS-positive continuum Schwinger functions, mediated by convergence of every finite reflected quadratic form and by a collar-collapse map \(F_i^\delta\to F_i\).
- same-theory condition: the collar limit may only implement diagonal congruences already included in the chosen renormalization of the locked observable algebra; no pair-dependent boundary state, cusp factor, or boundary counterterm may change the OS matrix entries.

## Assumptions

- For each fixed collar \(\delta>0\), RG convergence is strong enough that \(S_{a,L}(\Theta F_i^\delta,F_j^\delta)\to S_\delta(\Theta F_i^\delta,F_j^\delta)\) for all finite Wilson families and coefficients.
- The fixed-collar continuum matrices \(N^\delta_{ij}=S_\delta(\Theta F_i^\delta,F_j^\delta)\) are positive semidefinite by closed-cone transfer of regulated reflection positivity.
- After additive contact terms are removed or shown to vanish, the collar-collapse limit has the form \(N_{ij}=B_{ij}M_{ij}\), where \(M_{ij}=S(\Theta F_i,F_j)\) is the intended same-theory OS matrix.
- The anomaly kernel \(B\) is normalized by diagonal trace continuity, \(B_{ii}=1\), and is positive as a boundary Gram matrix of possible hairpin boundary states.

## Equations

- fixed-collar OS matrix: `N^\delta_{ij}=S_\delta(\Theta F_i^\delta,F_j^\delta),\qquad \sum_{i,j}\overline c_i c_j N^\delta_{ij}\ge 0`. Valid when: The observable supports are separated from the reflection plane by \(\delta>0\), the regulated lattice reflection positivity is available, and RG convergence controls this finite reflected probe.
- multiplicative boundary anomaly: `N_{ij}=\lim_{\delta\downarrow0}N^\delta_{ij}=B_{ij}M_{ij},\qquad M_{ij}=S(\Theta F_i,F_j)`. Valid when: Additive boundary contacts have vanished or been separated, and collar collapse is encoded by a normalized pair factor on the same Wilson core.
- reciprocal Schur transfer: `M=B^{[-1]}\circ N,\qquad B^{[-1]}_{ij}=1/B_{ij}`. Valid when: Every \(B_{ij}\neq0\) and the intended matrix is recovered from the collar matrix without changing the observable algebra.
- rank-one obstruction test: `B\ge0,\ B_{ii}=1,\ B^{[-1]}\ge0\ \Longrightarrow\ |B_{ij}|=1\ \forall i,j\ \Longrightarrow\ B_{ij}=u_i\overline{u_j}`. Valid when: The bridge is required to be valid as a Schur-positive operation on all finite OS matrices generated by the locked core.
- logarithmic cross-ratio: `K_{ik;jl}=\log B_{ij}+\log B_{kl}-\log B_{il}-\log B_{kj}`. Valid when: A consistent branch of the logarithm is chosen on a nonzero finite anomaly matrix; \(K=0\) is equivalent to factorization \(B_{ij}=u_i\overline{u_j}\) locally.

## Attempted Derivation

- Start with regulated reflection positivity: for every cutoff \((a,L)\), every positive-time gauge-invariant finite family, and coefficients \(c_i\), the quadratic form \(Q_{a,L}=\sum_{i,j}\overline c_i c_j S_{a,L}(\Theta F_i,F_j)\) is nonnegative.
- Restrict first to a fixed collar \(F_i^\delta\subset\{t>\delta\}\). The RG-to-OS topology is assumed to control each reflected matrix entry, so \(S_{a,L}(\Theta F_i^\delta,F_j^\delta)\to N^\delta_{ij}\). Since the positive semidefinite cone of finite matrices is closed, \(N^\delta\ge0\). This part is valid and essentially formal.
- Let \(\delta\downarrow0\). Decompose the difference between the collar limit and the intended boundary-trace matrix as \(N=M+A\) or, after eliminating additive positive contacts, as \(N=B\circ M\). Diagonal trace continuity sets \(A_{ii}=0\) and \(B_{ii}=1\).
- For additive positive \(A\), Cauchy-Schwarz gives \(|A_{ij}|^2\le A_{ii}A_{jj}=0\), hence \(A=0\). Therefore positive additive contacts cannot be the remaining obstruction.
- For multiplicative \(B\), recovering the intended same-theory matrix requires applying the entrywise reciprocal Schur multiplier: \(M=B^{[-1]}\circ N\). To preserve positivity for arbitrary finite collar matrices, \(B^{[-1]}\) must be positive semidefinite by the Schur product theorem criterion.
- Assume the anomaly is itself a normalized boundary Gram matrix, \(B\ge0\), \(B_{ii}=1\). For every two-by-two principal minor, \(B\ge0\) gives \(|B_{ij}|\le1\), while \(B^{[-1]}\ge0\) gives \(|1/B_{ij}|\le1\), so \(|B_{ij}|=1\). A normalized Gram matrix with all pairwise correlations of modulus one has all boundary vectors collinear, hence \(B_{ij}=u_i\overline{u_j}\) and \(K_{ik;jl}=0\).
- If \(B_{ij}=u_i\overline{u_j}\), then \(B^{[-1]}_{ij}=\overline{u_i}u_j\) and \(M=D^*ND\) for a diagonal unitary/congruence \(D\). Thus positivity transfers without changing the theory. If any normalized off-diagonal has modulus below one, the reciprocal multiplier has an indefinite two-by-two minor, giving an explicit falsifier.

Blocking gap: The derivation does not prove the Yang-Mills analytic input that reflected Wilson collar collapse produces either no multiplicative anomaly or only a rank-one boundary Gram anomaly. Balaban-style compactness and fixed-collar convergence do not by themselves exclude a nontrivial boundary Hilbert sector generated by coincident reflected hairpins. The missing estimate is \(C_\partial=0\), equivalently \(K=0\), for the connected boundary OPE of mixed reflected Wilson products in the locked observable algebra.

## Falsifiers

- A nonzero logarithmic cross-ratio \(K_{ik;jl}\) for four reflected Wilson hairpins with the same reflection-plane footprint; this is the cleanest multiplicative same-theory falsifier.
- A normalized boundary Gram matrix \(B\) with \(B_{ii}=1\) but \(|B_{ij}|<1\) for some mixed Wilson pair, since the reciprocal Schur multiplier is then indefinite.
- A boundary Wilson OPE producing a new reflection-plane observable whose coefficient is pair-dependent rather than a diagonal Wilson-loop renormalization.
- An indefinite additive contact term with zero diagonal but nonzero mixed entry; this would evade the positive-contact vanishing lemma and signal a deeper failure of the boundary trace model.
- RG convergence that is valid for individual Wilson expectations but not for reflected OS matrix entries \(S(\Theta F_i^\delta,F_j^\delta)\), especially when \(\Theta F_i^\delta\) and \(F_j^\delta\) collide into a hairpin.
- Gauge fixing, quotienting, or Wilson-loop renormalization that changes the locked test algebra between cutoff and continuum; positivity might survive, but for the wrong theory.

## Learning

Cycle 192 converts the remaining obstruction into an exact boundary Gram criterion. The formal reflection-positive closed-cone passage is valid on fixed collars, and positive additive contacts vanish once diagonal trace continuity is known. The only robust same-theory obstruction is a multiplicative boundary Gram factor from reflected Wilson hairpins. If its reciprocal Schur multiplier is to preserve OS positivity, two-by-two minors force the normalized Gram factor to be rank one; equivalently \(K=0\) and no nontrivial boundary Hilbert sector is created. Thus the next Yang-Mills work is no longer to restate reflection positivity, but to prove boundary Gram triviality \(C_\partial=0\) on the locked Wilson core or find a concrete nonrank-one hairpin falsifier.
