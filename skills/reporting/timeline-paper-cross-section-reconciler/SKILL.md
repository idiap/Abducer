# Timeline Paper Cross-Section Reconciler

## Mathematical Invariants

Keep symbols, definitions, assumptions, theorem/proposition names, and validation conditions consistent across sections. The mathematical object in the introduction must match the formal derivation and contribution statement.

## Epistemic Discipline

Cross-section coherence is not just style. It protects epistemic status by ensuring claims remain source-backed, conjectural claims are marked, proof-obligations are not hidden, and demotion or quarantine decisions are visible.

Every reconciliation finding must cite evidence: source artifacts, section IDs, source-unit IDs, reference IDs, formula labels, or explicit unknowns. Do not repair prose by intuition alone.

## Output Contract

Return: duplicate definitions, conflicting claims, dangling references, accepted claims, demoted claims, unresolved obligations, narrative-budget findings, section repair actions, epistemic status, and validation handoff.

## Failure/Demotion

Demote or block rendering if sections disagree on the mathematical object, if a claim is stronger in the conclusion than in the derivation, if references are decorative, if formulas lack assumptions, or if raw chronology exceeds the narrative budget.
